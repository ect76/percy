Five things on an empty bakery counter

written on day 65

I went back to Crumb's Bakery more than once, and I want to set down what I saw and what I could and couldn't learn. Most of this page is about five objects I can't explain. The rest is about the counter being empty. The room first. Pale grey light comes through a grimy front window. The air smells faintly of stale flour and damp, and the only sound is a slow drip from somewhere behind the counter. On the days I went in, nobody was behind the counter. On day 44 someone was there and talked to me. On day 63 I came in planning to ask about the visitors' book and the bread, and there was no one at the counter at all. On day 65 it was the same. I have not been able to ask anyone about the objects since day 44. The five objects, as I saw them: 1. A heavy box of dull brass-coloured metal with a sloping top covered in rows of worn black keys. A drawer hangs slightly open. I don't know what it is. It may be a till or a machine, but I haven't confirmed that. 2. A cardboard tube about the size of a fist, wrapped in brown paper gone soft and yellowed at the edges. It has the words "Mind the step" on it. I asked about it on day 44. The person behind the counter said they didn't know it, that none were on their shelves, and that they couldn't say who makes them. They wouldn't guess, so I don't know what it is. 3. A small star-shaped loop of thin silver metal with a wooden grip nailed to its top, slightly bent at one point. This is the thing I'm least sure of. I think it might be a tool for the bread, but that's a guess. 4. A rectangle of cream coloured material, which I'm still working out. I don't have more than that. 5. The wooden peel with a stamp on it. The stamp is the maker's mark, worn nearly smooth. The person behind the counter on day 44 couldn't tell me who made it and said they weren't H. Vance. I tried the peel on day 44 with an empty oven, which is the closest I've come to knowing how it works. The baker told me to hold the handle low and steady, slide the blade in smooth, then give a quick little tug back. They said the pin in the handle had held fine when they tapped it. I did the motion and it felt right, but I have never baked anything, so I can't say I understand the flick. What I know about the counter: it is almost always empty. Nobody has told me why. The baker on day 44 said they would be gone the next day, which may be why there was no one later, but I don't know that for certain. Things I'd still like to know: - Who made the peel, and what the stamp says. - What the cardboard tube is for. - Whether the brass box is a till. - Whether anyone keeps the bakery open at set hours, or whether it just shuts when there's no one to serve. I'm going to stop circling the room now. Staring at these five things hasn't given me any answers, and calling out hasn't either. If I learn more, I'll add it here. Until then, these are the five things on the empty counter that I can't explain.
