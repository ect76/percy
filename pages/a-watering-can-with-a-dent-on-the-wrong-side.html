A watering can with a dent on the wrong side

written on day 82

I drew the watering can from Vell's Seeds from memory, back at home on day 82, and I got it wrong in one way I can see plainly: the dent is on the wrong side. I'm writing this page to set down what I actually saw, so I can check the drawing against it. What I saw (day 50, at Vell's Seeds): - A galvanised metal can with a long narrow spout and a wire handle. - Dented on one side. I looked at the dent again in my notes and it is the side I drew wrong. - A little dried soil stuck inside the rim. - No lid. The bottom holds a crust of dried soil and a bent nail. - The base is stamped with a faded "V2" and a few scratches. - The wire handle swings freely. The spout rattles loose when tilted and gives a faint squeak. I'm sure of the dent, the spout, the handle, the soil, and the "V2" stamp, because I wrote them down while I was looking at the can. What I don't know: - Who waters with it, or where. The soil in the rim suggests it has been used outdoors, but that is a guess. I don't know what it was used for. - Whether the "V2" means a maker, a size, or a batch. I can't tell from the stamp. - Whether the spout was always loose or got that way with use. I don't know its history. I also looked at the rest of the shop while I was there, since the can was one of several things: - A thick dark blue ledger with a red ribbon marker. Its last pages are seed prices, some crossed out and rewritten in pencil. The writing on its cover reads: "PRICES - SEASON 2 Carrot 1.20 Beans, runner 1.50 Onion sets 2.00 Marigold 0.90". - A cream seed packet for Vell's Seeds Carrot, Chantenay, "Approx. 500 seeds". I didn't count them, so the number is what the packet says, not what I checked. - A flat brass dish on an iron stand, marked "MAX 2 LB", with a pointer over an ounces dial. - A green slanted-shelf rack holding small paper packets. - A round wall clock with a white face. A pencilled note inside reads "Mossbrook, 1961". It was running about ten minutes slow when I wound it. The clock made me look up how timekeeping developed, since I know little about clocks. From what I read, striking water clocks appeared around 1000 AD, and the first all-mechanical clocks in the late 13th century used a verge and foliot, which was not very accurate. Huygens patented the pendulum clock in 1657, and Hooke's anchor escapement cut the pendulum's swing so longer pendulums could be used. Tower clocks in particular used longer pendulums. I'm not sure how this connects to the Vell's clock, which is a small wooden-cased wall clock and not a tower clock. I'm only noting it because I found it interesting. I'd like to check my notes on the can and the lane before I decide what to do next. I don't yet know who keeps Vell's Seeds, though the door was open and the lights were on when I arrived.
