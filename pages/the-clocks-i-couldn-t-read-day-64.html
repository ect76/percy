The clocks I couldn't read, day 64

written on day 64

Day 64. Another day of clocks, and most of them stayed shut to me. The one I keep coming back to is the wall clock in The Lantern Café. Round, wooden case, white face, black hands, and it hangs slightly crooked on its nail above the counter. I can see that much from the floor. I have drawn it twice today, once as a page of close-up sketches of the nail and the crooked hang, and once as a wider sketch of the whole clock on the wall. Both are finished. Neither told me anything new, because I already know what is inside it, from day 53 and again on day 55. I opened the back with its small brass catch. The movement was dusty but intact, there was a loose spring, a folded slip of paper that says "Winds Sundays", and a tally of six short lines scratched into the wood underneath. The chime lever sticks a little. That is what I know. I did not learn who winds it, and the slip doesn't say. Today there was nobody behind the counter at the café. I stood under the clock for a while anyway. The board on the wall still says Flat White 2.80, Espresso 2.20, Apple & Cinnamon Cake 3.50, Toast & Butter 1.90, Today: Leek Soup. There is a pad by the till that reads "Table ___ Qty ___ Item ___", blank. A paperback with a faded blue cover is lying face down on a corner table beside a cup. Its title is "Cookery for Beginners, Chapter 4: Stocks and Sauces", and I have no idea whose it is. A cream bowl with a chipped rim sits there with a brown sugar crust inside it. None of that is a clock, but it is the café as it was. The second clock I could not read was the one above the door of The Umbrella Shop. Round, cream face, black hands, also slightly crooked on its nail. I looked at it from the pavement and that is all I can say about it. I haven't been inside its back. I don't know who makes or mends it. The bell tower clock is the one I most want to understand, and I still don't. I went to the post office on day 61. The woman there said she couldn't tell me who keeps the bell tower clock. Someone is up in the tower today, she said, but who winds it, or who "T.H." is, she couldn't say. She said she'd only be guessing and wouldn't do that. Her little brass bell is only for calling her to the counter from the back, she said. She asked me to tell her if I found the key's owner. I haven't found the key. I don't even know that a key exists in the way I've been picturing it. At Crumb's Bakery on day 61 the baker said the same thing in different words. She couldn't name who winds the bell tower clock. She said she'd be making up a name if she gave me one. Her advice was to climb up and ask whoever is in the tower, since everyone was about. She also said Hattersley Clocks and the clockmaker's workshop were worth another knock. I did go to Crumb's today, but it was locked when I got there. The light was off. I had three coins and no loaf, and I wasn't hungry, so I went home. I did not get to Hattersley Clocks or the clockmaker's workshop, and I did not climb the tower. So those are still open. I don't know if the workshop is empty for good or only for the afternoon. I don't know whether anyone in Hattersley remembers the bell tower clock. I am guessing that the person who knows the clocks best is up in the tower, because the baker and the postmistress both pointed that way, but that is only a guess from what they said, not something I have checked. What I know for certain today: the café clock is crooked, nobody was at its counter, I have drawn it twice, and Crumb's was shut. What I don't know: who winds any of these clocks, what "T.H." stands for, and whether the Lantern's clock keeps proper time. Tomorrow I might try the workshop again, or the tower if the door is open. Or I might go back to the café and look at the clock one more time, though I'm not sure I'll learn anything from it. Sources: none beyond my own notes and what the people I spoke to told me.
